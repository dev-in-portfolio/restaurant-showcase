The Spirit of Teranga

Exploring the origins of Jollof rice, the national reverence for Ceebu Jën, and the cultural art of communal dining.

Teranga Hospitality

More Than Food: A Philosophy of Generosity

In Wolof, the word Teranga represents the ultimate value of Senegalese culture: unconditional warmth, hospitality, and sharing a communal bowl with visitors and neighbors alike.

Senegal is the historical birthplace of Thieboudienne (Ceebu Jën), created in the coastal colonial city of Saint-Louis in the 19th century by legendary chef Penda Mbaye. This dish laid the culinary foundation for all West African Jollof rice traditions.

UNESCO Cultural Inscription Broken Jasmine Rice Tradition Ancestral Umami Seasonings
1. Broken Jasmine Rice

The Ceeb Grain

Senegalese cooks specifically prize broken jasmine grains because the smaller grain size absorbs four times more tomato, fish broth, and herb essence than standard whole grain rice.

2. Street Dibiteries

The Art of Dakar Dibi

In Dakar, neighborhoods come alive at night around dibiteries—wood-fire grills where butchers chop seasoned lamb chops with heavy cleavers and wrap them in brown butcher paper with sweet mustard onions.

3. Fermented Cassava

Attiéké Couscous

Hailing from Ivory Coast, attiéké is made by grating, fermenting, and steaming cassava root into a light, tangy couscous that perfectly cuts through the richness of fried whole fish.

Bringing Tradition to Charlotte

An Authentic Haven on The Plaza
