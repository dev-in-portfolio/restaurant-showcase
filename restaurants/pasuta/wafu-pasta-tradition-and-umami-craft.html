Japanese Culinary Culture • 和風パスタ

Shibuya Origins, Umami Emulsification.

Wafu pasta—literally "Japanese-style pasta"—was born in the post-war culinary landscape of Tokyo. In 1953, chef Takayasu Shigetomi opened Kabenoana in Shibuya and began dressing Italian spaghetti with soy sauce, butter, and seafood, pioneering a culinary tradition now celebrated globally.

Culinary Tenets

The Four Pillars of Wafu Saucing

How traditional Japanese pantry staples transform Italian pasta into an umami feast.

Pillar 01

Soy Butter Emulsion

Instead of heavy flour-thickened roux, Wafu pasta utilizes starchy pasta boiling water vigorously tossed with chilled butter and Japanese shoyu to create a glossy, clinging glaze.

Pillar 02

Mentaiko Cod Roe

Spicy cured pollock or cod roe marinated with chili and sake. When folded into warm noodles, millions of microscopic pearls burst with salinity, heat, and rich oceanic savoriness.

Pillar 03

Kizami Nori & Shiso

Finely shredded toasted nori seaweed and aromatic shiso mint leaves crown each bowl, imparting toasted roasted aromas and herbal freshness on every forkful.

Earthy Balance

Miso & Shimeji Mushroom Butter

Another classic Wafu pairing is sweet fermented white miso butter with Japanese shimeji beech mushrooms. The natural sweetness of the fermented rice koji in the miso rounds out the earthy mushroom caps, creating a comforting sauce that bridges rustic Italian peasant cuisine and Kyoto kaiseki sensibilities.
