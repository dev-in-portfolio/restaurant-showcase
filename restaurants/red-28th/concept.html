A Charlotte Cultural Landmark

The Harlem Salon, Reborn in Charlotte.

When Red@28th first unlocked its doors in 2012 on 28th Street in North Charlotte, founder Derrick Featherstone set out to build something the Queen City had never seen: a living-room sanctuary where literature, diverse creative minds, board games, soothing hookah smoke, and soulful hospitality could flourish without pretense.

Guiding Philosophy

The Four Tenets of Red@28th

Rooted in Black cultural heritage, open to every respectful soul who values community and art.

Tenet 01

The Living Room Ethos

Red@28th was designed to feel like your smartest, warmest friend's living room. No velvet ropes, no pretentious attitude—just deep couches, Persian rugs, vintage lamps, and an environment that fosters genuine conversation.

Tenet 02

Literary Freedom & Expression

Our shelves hold thousands of volumes—from James Baldwin, Toni Morrison, and Maya Angelou to global histories, poetry, and philosophy. We encourage patrons to read, discuss, borrow, and contribute books to our evolving library.

Tenet 03

Multicultural Inclusivity

True to our founding mission, our patrons represent the rich tapestry of Charlotte: creatives, university students, writers, entrepreneurs, and neighbors from all walks of life sharing tables and swapping perspectives.

A Resilient Legacy

From NoDa to University City

From our historic beginnings on 28th Street in NoDa to our vibrant chapter in Dilworth, and now anchored at 3509 David Cox Road in University City, Red@28th remains an enduring fixture in Charlotte's creative ecosystem. The address may evolve, but the sacred flame of culture, spoken word, soothing shisha, and warm fellowship remains unchanged.
