The Story, As Verified

Since 1945

The Diamond's history is documented by historic-preservation groups, neighborhood institutions, and the diner's own telling: a snug brick building on Commonwealth Avenue, a family named James, and a red neon sign that's been part of the neighborhood longer than most of its houses.

The Timeline

Eight decades, one corner.

1945

The Diamond Soda Grill Opens

Flonnie and W.A. James conceive the snug brick diner on Commonwealth Avenue — originally the Diamond Soda Grill — when Commonwealth ran directly off Independence Boulevard and the neighborhood was mill workers, mechanics, and young families.

The James Years

Mrs. James, The Beating Heart

Flonnie James, a Marshville, NC native who loved to cook and serve, made a point of learning her regulars. "We had people who came and ate with us every day," her son Ralph recalled — the origin of the regulars' counter the diner still runs on.

The Neon Era

A Sign You Can Navigate By

The large red neon sign gave the little diner visibility from busy Independence Boulevard a block away — enough of a landmark that Charlotte's historic roadside survey documents it by name.

Plaza Midwood Arrives

The Neighborhood Changes Around It

As Plaza Midwood turned eclectic — breweries, tattoo shops, new kitchens along Central and Commonwealth — the Diamond kept its booths, its counter, and its prices, picking up a reputation as the constant on a changing block.

Today

Eight Decades On The List

Eater Carolinas counts the Diamond among Charlotte's oldest restaurants and among North Carolina's best diners; The Infatuation notes it's still cranking out burgers and fried pork chops 80 years after opening. The Greek-leaning plates, the jukebox, the patio, and the banana pudding all made the cut into the modern era.
