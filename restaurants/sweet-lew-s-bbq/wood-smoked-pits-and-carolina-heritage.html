Traditional North Carolina Pit Smoking

Burning Down Logs, Shoveling Live Coals.

Real barbecue is not cooked with a dial. At Sweet Lew’s BBQ, we practice the laborious, time-honored craft of whole-wood pit smoking. Behind our Belmont smokehouse, split logs of seasoned hickory, white oak, and pecan are reduced to glowing embers in outdoor burn barrels, then shoveled directly beneath our barbecue pits throughout the night.

Pit Standards

The Four Rules of The Sweet Lew Pit

Why authentic wood smoking cannot be replicated with electricity or gas.

Rule 01

Zero Gas or Electric Assist

Most modern barbecue relies on automated gas or electric heating elements. At Sweet Lew’s, heat and smoke come solely from burning hardwood timbers.

Rule 02

Tri-Wood Hardwood Blend

We blend white oak for steady BTUs, mountain hickory for classic Southern pungent smoke, and North Carolina pecan for gentle sweet aromatics.

Rule 03

Eastern & Western Heritage

We respect both Carolinian traditions: our chopped pork features the sharp pepper-vinegar punch of Eastern NC, while our ribs offer Western Piedmont dip.

Heritage Technique

The Burn Barrel Ritual

Every morning before dawn, our pit crew fires up the burn barrels behind the kitchen. As logs burn down to red-hot embers, shovelfuls are transferred beneath the pits. This intense radiant heat renders intramuscular fat, creates a pristine smoke ring, and infuses genuine wood flavor into every fiber of our pork and brisket.
