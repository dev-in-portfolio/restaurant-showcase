800-Degree Radiant Cooking

The Sacred Fire of The Clay Tandoor.

The tandoor is the heart of North Indian gastronomy. A cylindrical bell of hand-thrown terracotta clay heated from below by natural hardwood coals, it combines convection, radiation, and direct conduction to create succulent roasts impossible to replicate in modern metal ovens.

The Four Elements of Tandoori Mastery

Every skewer reflects traditional technique, precise heat control, and unhurried craftsmanship.

Element 01

Cylindrical Clay Convection

Thick terracotta walls absorb radiant heat from bottom hardwood coals, sustaining steady 750°F to 850°F interior temperatures. Air circulates up the curved walls, searing meat surfaces in minutes while sealing moisture within.

Element 02

Two-Stage Yogurt Marinade

Meats first rest in ginger, garlic, lime juice, and salt to tenderize. Then a second marinade of thick whole-milk yogurt, mustard oil, crushed kasoori methi, and Kashmiri red chili creates a velvety protective crust.

Element 03

Vertical Skewer Smoking

Meats are threaded onto long square-steel skewers and lowered vertically into the tandoor. Natural meat juices and butter baste drip onto the glowing coals below, vaporizing instantly into rich aromatic smoke that perfumes the meat.

The Royal Spice

Pure Saffron (Kesar): The Soul of Our Craft

Our restaurant takes its name from saffron—the hand-harvested crimson stigmas of the purple crocus flower. Saffron imparts not just a brilliant sunshine-golden hue, but a delicate, honey-hay floral aroma that defines royal Mogul curries, saffron butter naan, and slow-simmered basmati rice at Saffron Indian Cuisine.
