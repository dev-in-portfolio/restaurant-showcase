Reel 05 · The Story

A café that started as a documentary.

The ChangeBaker Place takes its name from "The Changebaker," the short documentary about Manuel "Manolo" Betancur — baker, community organizer, and the man behind Manolo's Bakery. The café is that film's idea, built in real life on Central Avenue. Run the reel.

I.

The baker next door

Manolo's Bakery has fed East Charlotte from Central Avenue for years — Latin pastries, breads, and cakes from a family business that became a neighborhood institution. The ChangeBaker Place opened in March 2026 in the suite right next door, a former hair salon flipped into a café.

II.

The film

"The Changebaker" documents Betancur's work and the bakery's role in the community. The café's own pages describe the space as inspired by the award-winning documentary — the name on the door is the film's name.

III.

The year the bakery became a hub

In November 2025, Betancur briefly closed Manolo's Bakery over safety concerns when Border Patrol activity reached the neighborhood. The shop quickly became a focal point for local protests and organizing — national news covered a bakery that its neighborhood rallied around.

IV.

The nonprofit

Those outreach efforts were formalized as By Immigrant Hands, a nonprofit celebrating community, culture, and immigrant contributions in Charlotte. The ChangeBaker Place is designed as its real-world home base — the rooms in back exist for exactly that work.

V.

The living room

Betancur describes the place as equal parts coffee shop and civic living room: Colombian espresso and pastries at the counter, students at the communal table, organizers in the meeting rooms, and a daily pour that funds a bigger purpose.
