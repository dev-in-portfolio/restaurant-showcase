The Alchemy of Charred Chilies & Cacao.

In Oaxaca, mole and chocolate are not merely foods—they are sacred artistic traditions dating back over a millennium. Our kitchen carefully chars rare chilhuacle chilies, toasts native cacao beans, and simmers complex sauces with unhurried patience to create experiences that awaken the senses.

Culinary Principles

The Craft of Mole Negro & Cacao Rituals

Prepared without commercial pastes, bouillon cubes, or shortcuts.

Craft 01

Controlled Chili Charring

The signature ink-black hue and complex bitterness of Mole Negro comes from deliberately charring the seeds of chilhuacle negro, mulato, and pasilla oaxaqueño chilies on dry comals until ignited and turned to ash, then soaked in cool water to remove harshness.

Craft 02

Stone-Ground Mexican Chocolate

We use pure fermented cacao roasted and ground on volcanic stone metates with Ceylon cinnamon, roasted almonds, and cane sugar. In our Mole Negro, chocolate provides velvety mouthfeel and balances the smoky heat of the charred chilies.

Craft 03

The Molinillo Ritual

Every bowl of Chocolate Oaxaqueño is made to order by spinning a traditional hand-turned wooden molinillo between the palms. The fluted spheres whip the cacao oils and milk into a dense, creamy foam that holds sweet egg yolk bread.

UNESCO Heritage Gastronomy

Mole Negro: A Living Legend

Oaxacan cuisine is recognized globally by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, and Mole Negro is its undisputed crowning achievement. Infused with plantains, raisins, cloves, Mexican allspice, thyme, and sesame seeds, it delivers an extraordinary balance of sweet, smoky, bitter, and savory notes that linger on the palate long after the meal ends.
